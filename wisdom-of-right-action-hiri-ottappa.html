Before You Act: Let Your Conscience, Kindness and Wisdom Guide You

Understanding Hiri and Ottappa in Buddhist Teachings

Every day, we make decisions. Some are small and affect only our daily routines, while others influence our families, colleagues, friends, communities and the wider world.

When making a decision, it is easy to focus on what we can gain: money, recognition, convenience, popularity or personal advantage. We may also be influenced by what others expect us to do. Yet a decision that benefits us does not necessarily mean it is fair, kind or beneficial to everyone involved.

How, then, can we recognise a truly wholesome choice? Buddhist teachings offer two important qualities that can help guide our conduct: Hiri and Ottappa. They encourage us to examine our conscience, consider the consequences of our actions and take responsibility for how our choices affect others.

“Do not ask only what you can gain from a decision. Ask also who may be harmed, whether it is fair, and whether you can choose a kinder way.”

◈1. What Are Hiri and Ottappa?

In Buddhist teachings, Hiri and Ottappa are two important wholesome mental qualities that support moral conduct. They are often discussed together because both help prevent unwholesome actions.

Hiri – Moral Conscience

Hiri can be understood as moral conscience or a sense of moral shame. It is the inner awareness that makes us reluctant to do something wrong because it conflicts with our integrity, values and understanding of what is wholesome.

For example, we may have an opportunity to take credit for someone else's work. Nobody may notice, and we might even receive praise. However, our conscience reminds us that claiming another person's effort as our own is dishonest and unfair.

Hiri encourages us to respect ourselves by choosing honesty and integrity, even when nobody is watching.

Ottappa – Moral Concern About the Consequences of Wrongdoing

Ottappa is often explained as moral dread or a wise concern about the consequences of wrongdoing. It encourages us to consider the harm that unwholesome actions may bring to ourselves and others.

Before speaking harshly, making an unfair decision or taking advantage of someone, we pause to consider the possible consequences. Could our words hurt another person? Could our actions damage trust? Could someone suffer because we chose convenience over responsibility?

Ottappa is not simply fear of punishment or concern about being caught. It is a thoughtful awareness that our actions have consequences and that we should take responsibility for them.

Together, Hiri and Ottappa encourage us to avoid wrongdoing through inner conscience and careful consideration. They help us reflect before acting rather than waiting until harm has already occurred.

⟡2. Do Not Measure Every Decision by Personal Gain

In everyday life, we may sometimes judge a decision by asking: "What can I get out of this?" Although considering our own needs is reasonable, personal gain should not be the only measure of a good decision.

Imagine a situation in which someone can gain an advantage by withholding important information from another person. The decision may appear beneficial to the individual, but it may leave the other party at a disadvantage.

Is that truly a good outcome? If a choice creates benefits for one person while unfairly harming another, we should examine it more carefully.

A wholesome decision should consider the wellbeing, dignity and legitimate interests of everyone involved. Where possible, we should seek an outcome that is fair and beneficial to both parties rather than creating an unnecessary winner and loser.

This does not mean every situation can satisfy everyone. Sometimes responsibilities, needs or circumstances conflict. However, we can still act honestly, minimise harm and avoid taking unfair advantage of others.

❧3. Let Kindness and Generosity Be Part of the Decision

Kindness and generosity are important qualities in a wholesome life. They encourage us to look beyond our immediate interests and recognise that other people also have feelings, needs and difficulties.

When making a decision, we can ask whether there is a way to treat others with consideration. Can we share fairly? Can we communicate honestly? Can we offer help when appropriate? Can we avoid causing unnecessary difficulty for someone simply because doing so benefits us?

Generosity is not limited to giving money or material things. We can also give our time, attention, knowledge, encouragement and understanding.

A small act of consideration can make a meaningful difference. Listening patiently, acknowledging another person's contribution or giving someone a fair opportunity are everyday expressions of kindness.

Remember

Kindness does not mean agreeing with everyone, avoiding difficult conversations or allowing others to take advantage of us. We can maintain healthy boundaries while still being respectful, fair and compassionate.

◌4. Seven Questions to Ask Before Making a Decision

We do not always have the time to analyse every small choice in detail. However, when a decision may affect another person or have significant consequences, a brief pause can help us act more wisely.

Consider asking yourself these seven questions:

  1. Is my intention wholesome?
    Am I acting with honesty and goodwill, or am I mainly motivated by greed, anger, jealousy or personal advantage?
  2. Could my decision harm someone?
    Have I considered the possible effects on other people, including those who may not be able to speak for themselves?
  3. Is this decision fair?
    Am I treating everyone involved with respect, honesty and reasonable consideration?
  4. Can the outcome benefit both parties?
    Is there a way to reach an understanding that respects the needs and dignity of everyone involved?
  5. Am I being honest and transparent?
    Would I be comfortable explaining my decision openly without hiding important information?
  6. Would I accept the same treatment?
    If another person made this decision towards me, would I consider it reasonable and fair?
  7. Have I considered kindness and generosity?
    Is there a more considerate way to handle this situation without neglecting my own responsibilities?

These questions are not meant to make every decision complicated. They are reminders to pause, examine our intentions and consider the wider effects of our actions.

⚖5. Choosing What Is Right Even When It Is Unpopular

Sometimes, doing what is right may not be the easiest or most popular choice. We may face pressure from friends, colleagues, family members or a wider group to follow a decision that does not feel fair or wholesome.

We might remain silent when someone is treated unfairly because speaking up could create disagreement. We might participate in an unkind conversation because everyone else is doing so. We might agree to something questionable because refusing could affect our position or reputation.

Hiri encourages us to listen to our conscience. Ottappa reminds us to consider the consequences of going along with something harmful.

This does not mean we should act impulsively or assume that our first opinion is always correct. We should examine the facts, listen to different perspectives and respond with patience.

When we have reflected carefully and recognise that an action is unwholesome, we should have the courage to choose a better course, even if it is not the most popular option.

◇6. Consider the Wider Consequences

Our decisions rarely exist in isolation. A choice made today may influence relationships, trust, working environments, families and communities over time.

For example, a dishonest action may bring a short-term advantage but weaken trust. An unfair decision may create resentment and damage cooperation. A careless word may remain in another person's memory long after we have forgotten saying it.

On the other hand, honesty can strengthen trust. Fairness can build cooperation. Kindness can encourage understanding. Responsible actions can create a more supportive environment for everyone.

Before acting, consider not only the immediate result but also the longer-term effects. Ask yourself whether your decision contributes to a more peaceful and respectful relationship.

✧7. Practise Hiri and Ottappa in Everyday Life

Moral awareness becomes more meaningful when we practise it in ordinary situations. We do not need to wait for a major ethical dilemma to develop these qualities.

At Home

Speak respectfully, acknowledge the efforts of family members and avoid taking their kindness for granted. When disagreements arise, try to understand one another rather than focusing only on being right.

At Work

Be honest about your contributions, respect other people's efforts and avoid spreading information that may unfairly damage someone's reputation. Consider the impact of decisions on colleagues and customers.

In the Community

Respect shared spaces, follow reasonable rules and consider how your behaviour affects others. Small acts of responsibility help create a more considerate community.

In Personal Relationships

Be mindful of promises, boundaries and the trust others place in you. If you make a mistake, acknowledge it and take responsibility rather than shifting the blame.

When Using Technology

Pause before sharing a message, photograph or comment. Consider whether the information is accurate, whether it may hurt someone and whether sharing it is necessary. Online actions also have real consequences.

8. A Simple Daily Reflection

At the end of each day, take a few quiet minutes to reflect on your actions. There is no need to judge yourself harshly. The purpose is to learn and become more aware.

Questions for Personal Reflection

  1. Did I act honestly today?
  2. Did I consider how my decisions affected others?
  3. Was I fair when dealing with people?
  4. Did I offer kindness or help where I could?
  5. Was there a moment when I ignored my conscience?
  6. What could I do more wisely tomorrow?

If you recognise a mistake, acknowledge it without unnecessary self-blame. Where possible, correct the mistake, make amends and learn from the experience.

9. Conscience, Responsibility and Inner Peace

When our actions are guided by honesty, fairness and kindness, we are more likely to maintain trust in our relationships and peace within ourselves.

This does not mean that every good decision will be rewarded or that everyone will agree with us. Sometimes, doing the right thing requires patience, courage and personal sacrifice.

Yet when we know that we have made a sincere effort to avoid harm and act responsibly, we can feel more at ease with our choices.

Hiri and Ottappa remind us that ethical conduct begins within. We should not depend only on rules, public opinion or the possibility of being caught. Our own conscience and understanding of consequences can guide us towards more responsible behaviour.

Conclusion: Let Wisdom Guide the Way

A good decision is not simply one that brings us the greatest personal benefit. It should also be examined in terms of intention, fairness, kindness and its effects on others.

Through Hiri, we learn to respect our conscience and recognise when an action conflicts with our values. Through Ottappa, we become more mindful of the harm and consequences that wrongdoing may bring.

When these qualities are supported by wisdom, kindness and generosity, they can help us make more thoughtful choices in our families, workplaces and communities.

We may not always find a perfect solution, and we may not always be able to please everyone. What matters is that we make a sincere effort to understand the situation, avoid unnecessary harm and treat others with dignity.

Before you act, pause and reflect. Let conscience remind you of what is right, let wisdom help you see the consequences, and let kindness guide how you treat others.

May our thoughts, words and actions become more wholesome, bringing greater understanding, trust and peace to ourselves and those around us.
