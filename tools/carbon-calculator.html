🌱 Plant-Based Eating and Nature

Our food choices affect more than our health. They also influence land use, water consumption, biodiversity and greenhouse-gas emissions. Understanding these connections can help us make thoughtful choices for ourselves and the environment.

🌍 1. How Food Choices Affect the Environment

Food production uses natural resources such as land, water and energy. Different foods have different environmental impacts, depending on how they are produced, processed, transported and consumed.

Livestock production can contribute to greenhouse-gas emissions through animal digestion, manure management, feed production and land-use changes. Some plant-based foods generally require less land and produce fewer emissions per unit of protein.

However, environmental impacts vary between farming systems, locations and production methods. It is useful to consider the wider food-production system rather than assuming every product has the same impact.

🌿 2. Comparing the Environmental Impact of Protein Sources

Protein is an important part of a balanced diet. It can come from animal foods such as beef, lamb, pork and poultry, as well as plant foods such as beans, lentils, peas and tofu.

The following figures are illustrative estimates of greenhouse-gas emissions per 100 grams of protein, based on global-average estimates reported in the research by Poore and Nemecek (2018), as presented by Our World in Data.

🌳 3. Food Production, Land and Biodiversity

Agriculture requires land for growing crops, raising livestock and producing animal feed. Changes in land use can affect forests, grasslands, wetlands and the habitats of wild animals.

Reducing pressure on land can help protect natural habitats. Plant-based foods such as beans, lentils and peas can be useful protein sources while generally requiring less land than many animal-based protein sources.

Sustainable food production also involves protecting soil, maintaining healthy ecosystems, using water responsibly and reducing food waste.

💧 4. Water and Food Production

Water is needed throughout food production. Crops need water to grow, animals need water to drink, and food processing also uses water.

The water footprint of a food depends on where and how it is produced. Rainfall, irrigation methods, climate and farming practices all matter. Therefore, it is important not to assume that every plant-based food has a lower water footprint in every situation.

Choosing seasonal foods where practical, avoiding unnecessary food waste and supporting responsible farming practices can contribute to more thoughtful resource use.

🥬 5. Plant-Based Foods as Part of Everyday Meals

Plant-based protein can be found in many familiar foods, including:

  • Beans, chickpeas and lentils
  • Green peas and other legumes
  • Tofu and tempeh
  • Soybeans and soy-based foods
  • Nuts and seeds
  • Whole grains, which also contribute some protein

In Malaysia, foods such as dhal, tauhu, tempeh, chickpeas and beans can be incorporated into everyday meals. Vegetables, fruits and whole grains can add fibre and a variety of nutrients.

A balanced diet should provide sufficient energy, protein, vitamins and minerals. People with specific nutritional needs should seek guidance from a qualified healthcare professional or dietitian before making major dietary changes.

🌏 6. Small Food Choices Can Support a Healthier Environment

Protecting nature does not always require dramatic changes. We can begin by paying more attention to what we eat, how much food we purchase and how much we waste.

  • Include a variety of vegetables, fruits and legumes in meals.
  • Explore plant-based protein options that suit your needs.
  • Plan meals to reduce unnecessary food waste.
  • Store food properly to help it last longer.
  • Support responsible food producers where practical.
  • Appreciate the connection between food, soil, water and nature.

Every household has different circumstances. The aim is not perfection, but greater awareness and thoughtful choices that can be maintained over time.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Does eating plant-based food always have a lower environmental impact?

Not necessarily in every individual case. Environmental impact depends on the type of food, how it is grown or produced, processing, transport and other factors. Many common plant-based protein sources have lower average greenhouse-gas emissions than many animal-based sources.

Are beans and lentils good sources of protein?

Yes. Beans, lentils, chickpeas and other legumes provide plant-based protein and fibre. They can be included in a varied and balanced diet.

Is tofu environmentally friendly?

Tofu generally has lower average greenhouse-gas emissions than many animal-based protein sources. Its overall impact still depends on soybean production, processing, energy use and other factors.

Does this calculator measure my complete carbon footprint?

No. It compares estimated emissions associated with a selected amount of protein. It does not include your entire diet, household energy, transport or other lifestyle activities.

Do I need to stop eating all animal products?

This article is intended to provide environmental information, not to prescribe one diet for everyone. People can learn about different food choices and decide what is appropriate for their health, circumstances and values.

🌱 A Thought to Take Away

Food connects us with the land, water, plants, animals and people who help sustain our lives. By understanding the environmental effects of our food choices, we can make decisions with greater awareness and appreciation for the natural world.

Small, thoughtful actions in everyday life can be part of a more sustainable relationship with nature.
