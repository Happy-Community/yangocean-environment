Can Eating More Plant-Based Foods Help Nature?

The food we choose every day can influence the environment. Producing food requires land, water, energy and other resources. Different foods also produce different amounts of greenhouse gas emissions throughout their supply chains.

Scientific research generally finds that plant-based foods such as vegetables, beans, peas and tofu have lower environmental impacts than many meat products, especially beef and lamb.

Choosing more plant-based meals and reducing our consumption of high-impact meats can be one practical way to care for nature. We do not need to make every meal perfect. Small, consistent changes can contribute to a more sustainable food system.

1. How Can Plant-Based Eating Help the Environment?

🌍 Reduce Greenhouse Gas Emissions

Livestock production can generate greenhouse gases such as methane and nitrous oxide. Cattle and sheep are particularly associated with higher emissions because of digestion, feed production and land requirements.

Replacing some meat meals with beans, peas, lentils or tofu may reduce the greenhouse gas emissions associated with our diets. The amount of reduction depends on the foods being replaced and the way those foods are produced.

🌳 Reduce Pressure on Land and Forests

Meat production requires land for grazing animals and for growing animal feed. In some regions, agricultural expansion contributes to deforestation and habitat loss.

Eating more plant-based foods can reduce the demand for land associated with livestock production. Protecting natural habitats helps support wildlife, biodiversity and healthy ecosystems.

💧 Use Resources More Efficiently

Food production requires water, energy and agricultural inputs. The environmental footprint varies considerably between crops and farming systems.

Many plant-based protein foods generally require fewer resources than high-impact meats. However, water use also depends on the crop, the climate and whether the area experiences water scarcity.

🐝 Support Biodiversity

Reducing pressure to expand agricultural land can help preserve forests, grasslands and other natural habitats. These habitats provide food and shelter for birds, insects, mammals and many other living organisms.

Sustainable farming practices, reduced pesticide use and protection of natural ecosystems are also important. Plant-based eating is one part of a broader effort to protect biodiversity.

Important: These are global averages, not exact footprints for every farm or meal. Actual impacts can vary with farming practices, land-use change, processing, transport and other factors. CO₂-equivalent includes the warming effects of different greenhouse gases.

4. Simple Ways to Eat More Sustainably

  • Try one or more meat-free meals each week.
  • Include beans, lentils, peas or tofu as protein sources.
  • Add a variety of vegetables to your meals.
  • Choose seasonal produce when practical.
  • Reduce food waste by planning meals and using leftovers.
  • Store food properly to keep it fresh for longer.
  • Support farmers who use responsible and sustainable practices.
  • Enjoy a balanced diet that meets your nutritional needs.

A plant-based meal does not have to be complicated. Rice with vegetables and lentils, tofu with leafy greens, or a bean-based soup can be simple and nourishing options.

5. A Mindful Choice for the Planet

Mindful eating invites us to pay attention not only to the food on our plates, but also to the resources and living systems connected to that food.

Choosing more plant-based meals can be a meaningful step toward reducing our environmental footprint. It can also encourage appreciation for the soil, water, plants, farmers and ecosystems that make our food possible.

We do not need to judge ourselves or others by their food choices. Instead, we can learn, make informed decisions and gradually develop habits that support both our well-being and the natural world.

Every mindful meal is an opportunity to care for ourselves and the Earth.
