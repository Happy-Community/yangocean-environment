🛍️ Shopping Impact Tool

A simple way to pause before buying and think about necessity, durability, reuse, repair, packaging and whether an item could be borrowed, shared or bought second-hand.

🛍️ Before You Buy

Shopping has an environmental impact beyond the moment of purchase. Materials, manufacturing, transport, packaging, use and disposal can all contribute to that impact. This tool encourages a short pause before buying so you can consider the whole life of a product.

Think about whether you already have something that serves the same purpose.

🌱 What Does the Score Mean?

80–100: Lower Impact Choice

The purchase appears to have several positive qualities, such as being necessary, durable, reusable, repairable or lightly packaged.

60–79: Moderate Impact

There are some good qualities, but you may be able to improve the choice by considering durability, packaging, reuse or another option.

40–59: Consider Again

A short pause may be useful. Ask whether you already own something similar or whether borrowing, repairing or buying second-hand could work.

0–39: Higher Impact Choice

Consider whether the purchase is necessary and whether a longer-lasting, reusable, repairable or lower-packaging alternative is available.

♻️ What Makes a Shopping Choice More Sustainable?

There is no single factor that determines whether a product is sustainable. Looking at several factors together can provide a more useful picture.

  • Necessity: Buying only what you genuinely need can reduce unnecessary resource use.
  • Durability: A product that lasts longer may reduce the need for repeated replacement.
  • Reuse: Products that can be used many times may reduce waste compared with single-use alternatives.
  • Repair: Repairing an item can extend its useful life and delay disposal.
  • Packaging: Choosing less packaging or reusable packaging can reduce material waste.
  • Second-hand and sharing: Borrowing, sharing, renting or buying used items can sometimes avoid the need for a new product.

🌍 Think Beyond the Purchase

A product's environmental impact can occur at many stages of its life. Raw materials may need to be extracted, products manufactured and transported, and packaging produced. After purchase, energy, water, maintenance and eventual disposal may also matter.

This does not mean that every purchase needs a complicated calculation. For everyday decisions, a few simple questions can be enough:

  • Do I really need this?
  • Do I already own something that can do the same job?
  • Will it last?
  • Can I repair it?
  • Can I reuse it?
  • Can I choose less packaging?
  • Could I borrow, share or buy it second-hand?

🌱 A More Mindful Way to Shop

Sustainable shopping is not about making every purchase perfect. It is about becoming more aware of the choices we make and gradually reducing unnecessary consumption.

Sometimes the most sustainable option is simply to use what we already have.

ℹ️ About This Tool

The Shopping Impact Score is a simple educational guide. It is not a scientific measurement of exact carbon emissions, ecological damage or the total environmental footprint of a product.

Actual environmental impacts vary according to materials, manufacturing methods, energy sources, transport, product lifespan, use, repair, packaging and end-of-life treatment.

Use the score as a prompt for mindful decision-making rather than as a precise environmental assessment.
