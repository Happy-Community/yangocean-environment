TCM Acupressure Points for Mindful Eating

A Traditional Practice for Everyday Wellness

Mindful eating is about more than choosing healthy foods. It also means slowing down, noticing hunger and fullness, enjoying each bite and building a more caring relationship with our bodies.

Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) includes practices such as acupuncture and acupressure. In acupressure, gentle pressure is applied to selected points on the body without inserting needles. Some people include this practice in their personal wellness routine.

This guide introduces four commonly discussed TCM points. Their traditional uses should not be confused with proven medical effects. Acupressure is optional and should complement—not replace—balanced nutrition or appropriate healthcare.

1. Why Explore Acupressure?

In TCM, specific points are traditionally associated with different body functions and patterns of wellbeing. Acupressure uses fingers to apply gentle pressure to these points.

Taking a short pause to breathe and notice how we feel can also support a mindful routine. If acupressure is meaningful to you, it can be one small part of that pause.

However, scientific evidence varies by condition. Research on acupuncture does not establish that pressing these points will reliably improve digestion, control appetite or produce weight loss.

Keep the Bigger Picture in Mind

  • Eat a variety of nutritious foods.
  • Notice hunger and fullness without judgement.
  • Drink water regularly.
  • Move your body in ways that suit you.
  • Seek medical advice for persistent symptoms.
A gentle reminder: There is no need to press any point to eat well or to be healthy. The most useful routine is one that is safe, comfortable and sustainable for you.

2. Four Commonly Discussed TCM Points

The locations below are simplified educational illustrations, not a substitute for professional instruction. Point locations may be described differently across teaching references. If you are unsure where a point is, ask a qualified TCM practitioner.

ST36 · Zusanli

Stomach 36

Traditional context: A commonly used point in TCM.

General location: On the front-outside of the lower leg, below the knee, near the outer side of the shinbone.

Traditional use: Often discussed in TCM in relation to digestive wellbeing and general vitality.

Remember: A diagram cannot precisely locate the point for every person.

LI4 · Hegu

Large Intestine 4

Traditional context: A point on the hand.

General location: On the back of the hand, in the fleshy area between the thumb and index finger. The precise point should be confirmed by a qualified TCM practitioner.

Traditional use: Commonly used in TCM practice for certain pain-related complaints.

Safety: Avoid stimulating LI4 during pregnancy unless specifically advised by a qualified healthcare professional.

SP6 · Sanyinjiao

Spleen 6

Traditional context: A point on the inner lower leg.

General location: On the inner lower leg, above the inner ankle bone, near the back edge of the shinbone.

Traditional use: Discussed in TCM for a range of concerns, including some digestive and reproductive complaints.

Safety: Avoid pressing this point during pregnancy unless advised by a qualified professional.

REN12 · Zhongwan

Conception Vessel 12

Traditional context: A point on the upper abdomen.

General location: On the midline of the upper abdomen, above the navel. Exact location should be confirmed by a trained practitioner.

Traditional use: Commonly associated with stomach-related concerns in TCM.

Safety: Do not press deeply on the abdomen, especially if you have pain, a recent operation, or a known abdominal condition.

3. How to Approach Acupressure Gently

If you choose to explore acupressure, treat it as a gentle complementary practice—not as a way to force the body to digest food, suppress hunger or lose weight.

  1. Wash your hands and sit in a comfortable position.
  2. Use a light touch on a comfortable area of skin.
  3. Never press hard enough to cause pain, numbness, bruising or tingling.
  4. Stop immediately if you feel uncomfortable or develop symptoms.
  5. Ask a qualified TCM practitioner for guidance if you are unsure about a point.
There is no universally established home acupressure schedule for healthy eating or weight loss. You do not need to stimulate all four points or follow a particular number of repetitions.

4. Mindful Eating: Everyday Habits That Matter

Before a Meal

  • Pause and take a few relaxed breaths.
  • Notice whether you feel hungry, thirsty, tired or stressed.
  • Choose a balanced meal that suits your needs and preferences.

During a Meal

  • Eat at a comfortable pace.
  • Chew comfortably and notice the taste and texture of your food.
  • Take breaks and notice feelings of fullness.
  • Allow yourself to enjoy food without guilt or judgement.

Build a Balanced Plate

Where practical, include a variety of:

  • Vegetables and fruit
  • Whole grains and other fibre-rich foods
  • Protein sources such as beans, eggs, fish or tofu
  • Healthy fats in appropriate amounts
  • Water and other suitable unsweetened drinks

Food needs differ between individuals. Adapt meals to your culture, health conditions, budget and nutritional needs.

After a Meal

  • Notice how you feel without judging yourself.
  • Consider gentle movement if it is comfortable for you.
  • Keep a regular eating routine that suits your daily life.
  • Seek medical advice if digestive discomfort persists or worsens.

5. Can Acupressure Help With Weight Management?

Acupuncture and related techniques have been studied for weight management, but study methods and results vary. Some research reports possible benefits, while limitations in the available evidence mean that firm conclusions cannot be drawn.

It is therefore best not to rely on acupressure as a weight-loss method or assume that pressing a point will reduce appetite, burn fat or change metabolism. For a weight goal, sustainable eating patterns, physical activity, adequate sleep and professional guidance are more appropriate foundations.

Weight is influenced by many factors, including genetics, medicines, sleep, stress, medical conditions and environment. A person's health cannot be judged by weight alone.

6. Safety and When to Seek Help

  • Do not use acupressure to delay diagnosis or treatment for a health problem.
  • If you are pregnant, have a chronic condition, take medication or have recently had surgery, ask a healthcare professional before trying unfamiliar body-point techniques.
  • Do not insert needles yourself. Acupuncture should be performed by a properly trained practitioner using sterile, single-use needles.
  • Stop if pressure causes pain, bruising, dizziness or other discomfort.
  • Severe or persistent abdominal pain, repeated vomiting, blood in the stool, unexplained weight loss or other worrying symptoms need medical assessment.

Health information notice: This article is for general educational purposes only. It is not personal medical advice, a diagnosis or a treatment plan. Traditional explanations and modern scientific evidence are not always the same. Consult a qualified healthcare professional for advice suited to your circumstances.
