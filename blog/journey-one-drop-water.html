💧 The Journey of One Drop of Water

From the sky, to the Earth, and back again

Look at this tiny drop of water.

It may seem insignificant.

But that little drop is part of a much bigger journey — a journey that has been happening on Earth for billions of years.

💧 One Small Drop

Watch this small drop of water as a reminder that something that looks insignificant can be part of something much bigger.

Countless drops together form rivers, lakes, groundwater and the water resources on which life depends.

☀️ The Water Cycle

The sun warms the Earth.

Water from the sea, rivers, lakes, soil and plants evaporates into the atmosphere. The invisible water vapour rises into the sky.

High in the atmosphere, the vapour cools.

Tiny droplets form around particles in the air. Millions of these droplets gather together to form clouds.

When the droplets become large and heavy enough, they fall as rain.

Some rain flows into rivers and lakes.

Some enters the soil and replenishes groundwater.

Some is taken up by plants.

Eventually, much of the water returns to the atmosphere again.

This continuous movement is called the water cycle.

💧 So Why Should We Conserve Water?

The water cycle does not mean that clean, usable freshwater is unlimited everywhere.

A drop of rain falling into the ocean cannot immediately become drinking water.

Water can be stored in oceans, glaciers, groundwater, rivers, lakes, soil and living organisms.

Only a small proportion of Earth's water is readily available as freshwater for human use.

At the same time, demand for freshwater continues to grow.

This means that protecting the freshwater we depend on is important, even though water itself continues to cycle through nature.

🚰 How Our Actions Affect Freshwater

Our everyday actions can affect how much usable freshwater is available.

🚰 Leaving taps running uses water unnecessarily.

🚿 Using more water than necessary increases demand.

🏭 Pollution can make freshwater resources more difficult to use safely.

🌳 Forests and watersheds play important roles in healthy water systems.

💧 Groundwater can decline when extraction is faster than natural recharge.

🌱 A Simple Water Tank Analogy

Imagine the water tank shown in this video.

One small drop seems almost meaningless. But imagine thousands of drops, then millions. Together, they contribute to the amount of water available.

Now imagine water continuously being taken from the tank while very little is allowed to return. The water level will gradually fall.

If water consumption is greater than the rate at which water is replenished, the available supply can decrease.

The same principle can apply to freshwater resources when extraction exceeds natural replenishment.

⚖️ Water Scarcity Is a Balance

The availability of freshwater depends on the balance between water replenishment, consumption, losses and pollution.

Water replenished by nature − Water we consume − Water lost or polluted = Water remaining available

If consumption and losses are greater than natural replenishment, the amount of available freshwater can decrease.

Water conservation is therefore not about stopping the natural water cycle. It is about using freshwater resources wisely while protecting the natural systems that replenish them.

🌧️ One Drop Is Small. Our Combined Actions Are Not.

The drop in this video is tiny.

But billions of people use water every day. Small individual choices can therefore add up to a large collective demand.

Water conservation can be simple:

🚰 Use only the water you need.

💧 Avoid unnecessary waste.

🏞️ Help keep rivers, lakes and other water sources clean.

🌳 Protect forests and watersheds that support healthy water systems.

🌱 Use freshwater resources in ways that allow natural replenishment to continue.

Use water wisely.
Keep water clean.
Protect the natural systems that support freshwater.

💙 Every Drop Matters

Water continuously moves through the natural water cycle. But clean and usable freshwater is a resource that needs to be protected.

Our everyday choices can contribute to greater water efficiency or unnecessary waste.

We may not be able to control where every drop goes. But we can choose how thoughtfully we use the water available to us.

Protect freshwater today — so that people, plants and animals can continue to depend on it tomorrow.
