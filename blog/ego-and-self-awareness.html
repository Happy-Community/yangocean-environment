Understanding Ego: Looking Beyond Pride and Inferiority

Have we ever felt the need to prove that we are better than someone else? Or, on the other hand, felt small when comparing ourselves with another person? Both experiences invite us to look more closely at the role of ego in our lives.

What Do We Mean by Ego?

In everyday life, ego often refers to the way we think about ourselves, our identity, our importance and our place in relation to others.

We may become attached to our achievements, knowledge, appearance, possessions, status or opinions. We may want others to recognise our worth, agree with us or admire us. When our self-image feels threatened, we may become defensive, dismissive or upset.

Having a sense of identity is part of ordinary human experience. The difficulty arises when we become so attached to the image we have of ourselves that we struggle to see situations and other people clearly.

When Ego Makes Us Feel Superior

Sometimes, we compare ourselves with others and conclude that we are more intelligent, more successful, more capable or more deserving of respect.

We may interrupt others because we believe our opinions matter more. We may look down on someone because they have less money, less education, a different background or fewer achievements. We may find it difficult to admit a mistake because doing so feels like losing face.

Yet every person has a life shaped by different experiences, opportunities, difficulties and circumstances. Being stronger in one area does not make us superior in every aspect of life.

When pride causes us to diminish others, we may gain a temporary feeling of importance, but lose an opportunity to understand them.

When Ego Makes Us Feel Inferior

Ego does not always appear as pride. It can also show itself through painful comparison.

We may feel inadequate when someone earns more, looks different, receives recognition or appears to be more successful. We may become overly concerned about how others judge us, or believe that our worth depends on their approval.

In these moments, we may forget our own strengths and the value of our individual journey. We may try to imitate others, hide our weaknesses or continually seek validation.

Recognising these feelings does not mean blaming ourselves. It gives us an opportunity to understand what is happening and respond with greater kindness towards ourselves.

The Habit of Comparing

Comparison can easily become a habit. We measure our achievements against another person's achievements, our appearance against theirs, or our circumstances against what we see in their lives.

But we rarely know the complete story behind another person's life. What we see may be only a small part of their experience.

Every person has different strengths, limitations, responsibilities and challenges. When we constantly compare, we may overlook the opportunities to learn, appreciate and grow that are already present in our lives.

A moment to reflect:

Instead of asking, “Am I better or worse than this person?”, we might ask, “What can I learn from this situation, and how can I respond with wisdom and kindness?”

Humility Is Not Self-Diminishment

Letting go of excessive pride does not mean denying our abilities or pretending that our achievements have no value. Nor does it mean accepting disrespect or believing that we are less worthy than others.

Humility allows us to recognise our strengths without looking down on others. It also allows us to acknowledge our limitations without losing confidence in our ability to learn.

We can be proud of meaningful work while remaining open to feedback. We can recognise another person's talent without feeling threatened. We can admit that we are wrong without believing that we have no worth.

When we no longer need every interaction to confirm our importance, relationships can become more open, respectful and genuine.

Observing Ego Through Mindfulness

Mindfulness can help us notice the thoughts, feelings and impulses that arise when our self-image is challenged. Instead of reacting immediately, we can pause and observe.

For example, when someone disagrees with us, we might notice the urge to defend ourselves. When another person succeeds, we might notice envy or insecurity. When we receive praise, we might notice the desire for more recognition.

These observations are not reasons to condemn ourselves. They are opportunities to understand our habitual responses. With awareness, we can choose whether a reaction is helpful before allowing it to shape our words and actions.

A simple practice is to pause when a strong reaction arises and quietly ask:

  • What am I feeling right now?
  • Am I trying to protect my pride or prove my worth?
  • Am I comparing myself with someone else?
  • Can I respond in a way that is fair and considerate?

We may not always respond perfectly. The practice is to recognise what is happening, learn from it and begin again.

From Self-Importance to Understanding

When we become less preoccupied with proving ourselves, we may have more space to listen carefully, acknowledge different perspectives and appreciate the contributions of others.

We can still pursue our goals, express our opinions and develop our abilities. The difference lies in whether we must place ourselves above others to feel valuable.

A more balanced relationship with ourselves allows us to recognise that every person deserves basic respect, regardless of status, wealth, appearance or achievement.

Rather than asking others to make us feel important, we can work towards becoming more aware, responsible and compassionate in the way we live.

Questions for Reflection

  • When do I feel the need to prove that I am right?
  • Do I sometimes judge people by their status, appearance or achievements?
  • When I compare myself with others, what feelings arise?
  • Can I recognise my strengths without feeling superior?
  • Can I acknowledge my limitations without feeling inferior?
  • What might change if I listened with greater openness and less defensiveness?

A Gentle Reminder

We do not need to be above others to have value, nor do we need to place ourselves below them.

When we observe pride, insecurity and comparison with greater awareness, we create an opportunity to meet ourselves and others with humility, understanding and kindness.

The journey begins not by judging our ego, but by learning to recognise it.
