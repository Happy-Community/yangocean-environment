Think Before You Believe: The Importance of Verifying Information

Inspired by the Wisdom of the Kalama Sutta

1. We Live in a World Full of Information

Every day, we receive information from countless sources. We listen to conversations, read messages, watch videos, browse social media and follow news reports. Information travels so quickly that we can encounter hundreds of different opinions and claims within a short time.

Some information is accurate and useful. Some is incomplete or misunderstood. Others may be misleading or deliberately presented to influence our emotions and opinions.

When we hear something repeatedly, we may gradually assume that it must be true. When someone speaks confidently, we may believe that the person must know what they are talking about. When many people share the same message, we may feel that it is reliable.

However, popularity does not necessarily establish truth. Confidence does not guarantee accuracy, and repetition does not turn an unverified claim into a fact.

In a world filled with information, one important habit we should develop is the ability to pause, examine and verify before forming a conclusion.

2. An Ancient Teaching with a Timely Message

More than 2,500 years ago, the Buddha offered guidance on how we should approach information and teachings that create uncertainty. This discourse is known as the Kalama Sutta, recorded in the Anguttara Nikaya 3.65 (AN 3.65) of the Theravada Buddhist tradition.

The Kalamas, who lived in a place called Kesaputta, encountered different teachers who presented conflicting teachings. Each teacher praised their own views while criticising those of others. Naturally, the Kalamas became confused and wondered whom they should believe.

The Buddha acknowledged that their uncertainty was understandable. He advised them not to accept something merely because it had been repeatedly heard, passed down through tradition, reported by others, recorded in scripture, reasoned out, or presented by a respected teacher.

Instead, he encouraged careful examination. We should consider whether something is wholesome or unwholesome, whether it leads to harm or wellbeing, and whether wise people would approve of it.

This teaching offers a valuable reminder for modern society. We should not surrender our judgement simply because something is familiar, widely accepted or confidently presented.

The Kalama Sutta does not mean that we should reject everything or trust only our personal opinions. Rather, it encourages thoughtful investigation, ethical reflection and sound judgement.

3. Do Not Believe Everything You Hear

Information often travels from one person to another. During this process, details may be forgotten, exaggerated, misunderstood or changed.

A story that begins as someone's personal experience may eventually be repeated as though it were an established fact. A rumour may spread widely even when nobody has checked its original source.

Before accepting something we hear, we can ask ourselves:

  • Who originally provided this information?
  • Is there a reliable source supporting the claim?
  • Has the information been taken out of context?
  • Are important details missing?
  • Can the claim be independently verified?

Listening carefully is important, but listening does not require us to believe everything we hear. We can respect the person speaking while remaining thoughtful about the information being shared.

4. Do Not Judge Only by What You See

Seeing something with our own eyes may feel convincing. Yet what we see is not always the complete picture.

A photograph may show only one moment. A video may omit what happened before or afterwards. An image may be edited, artificially generated or presented with a misleading description.

Even when something is genuine, our interpretation may not be accurate. We may see an action without understanding the circumstances behind it.

Mindfulness reminds us to distinguish between what we observe and what we assume. Instead of immediately concluding that we understand everything, we can acknowledge that there may be more to learn.

This attitude helps reduce misunderstanding, unfair judgement and unnecessary conflict.

5. Respect Others, but Examine Their Claims

We naturally place trust in people we admire: teachers, leaders, professionals, public figures and experienced individuals. Their knowledge and experience can be valuable.

Nevertheless, respect for a person should not prevent us from examining a particular statement or claim. Even knowledgeable people can make mistakes, have incomplete information or express personal opinions as though they were established facts.

We can appreciate someone's experience without surrendering our responsibility to think carefully. Similarly, asking questions does not necessarily mean disrespecting the person who made a statement.

A wise approach combines humility with discernment. We remain willing to learn, but we do not accept everything without examination.

6. Verify Before Sharing Information

Today, a single message can reach thousands of people within minutes. Sharing information has become easy, but verifying it requires attention and responsibility.

Before forwarding a message, posting a claim or repeating a story, we can develop a simple habit of checking the following:

  1. Find the original source. Look for the original report, research, statement or publication rather than relying only on a screenshot or forwarded message.
  2. Check the date and context. Old information may be presented as recent. A genuine photograph or statement may also be used in an unrelated context.
  3. Compare reliable sources. Where appropriate, check whether other credible and independent sources support the same claim.
  4. Distinguish fact from opinion. Personal experiences and interpretations can be meaningful, but they are not automatically evidence of a general claim.
  5. Be careful with health and safety claims. Check recognised medical or public health sources. Seek qualified professional advice when decisions could affect someone's wellbeing.
  6. Do not share when uncertain. It is perfectly acceptable to wait, investigate further or decide not to forward something.

These practical steps are modern ways of applying careful inquiry. They are not a list of instructions quoted directly from the Kalama Sutta, but they reflect the importance of examining claims rather than accepting them blindly.

7. Be Aware of Our Own Emotions and Assumptions

Sometimes the greatest obstacle to clear understanding is not the information itself, but our own minds.

We may readily believe information that agrees with our existing opinions and reject information that challenges them. We may become excited by a message that confirms our hopes, frightened by alarming news, or angry when someone expresses a different viewpoint.

When emotions become strong, we may react before thinking. We may overlook missing evidence, misunderstand another person's intentions or share information that later proves to be inaccurate.

Mindfulness gives us an opportunity to notice these reactions. We can observe our excitement, fear, anger or attachment without allowing them to determine what we accept as true.

We should also be willing to reconsider our conclusions when better evidence becomes available. Changing our minds after learning something new is not a weakness. It is part of developing wisdom.

8. Consider the Consequences of What We Believe

Verifying information is not only about discovering whether something is accurate. It is also important to consider how we use that information and what consequences may follow.

Does sharing this message help people understand a situation, or does it create unnecessary fear? Does it encourage kindness and understanding, or does it promote prejudice and hostility?

The Kalama Sutta encourages us to consider whether our actions are rooted in wholesome or unwholesome qualities, and whether they lead towards harm or wellbeing.

Good intentions alone do not make incorrect information true. Even when we genuinely want to help others, we should still check our facts. At the same time, accurate information should be communicated responsibly and with consideration for others.

Wisdom involves both careful understanding and responsibility for our actions.

9. A Simple Mindful Practice: Pause, Examine and Reflect

We do not need to become experts in every subject to practise discernment. We can begin with a few simple habits whenever we encounter information that seems important, surprising or emotionally disturbing.

  1. Pause. Avoid reacting immediately or forwarding information impulsively.
  2. Observe. Notice your thoughts and feelings. Are you excited, frightened, angry or eager to agree?
  3. Examine. Look for the original source, supporting evidence and relevant context.
  4. Reflect. Consider whether the information is reliable and whether acting upon it could cause harm.
  5. Respond wisely. Accept what is sufficiently supported, remain open where uncertainty exists, and avoid spreading claims that have not been verified.

This practice can be applied to everyday conversations, family discussions, workplace decisions, online messages and important personal choices.

10. Wisdom Begins When We Are Willing to Examine

The Kalama Sutta reminds us not to surrender our judgement merely because something is familiar, widely repeated, confidently presented or supported by authority.

At the same time, it does not encourage us to become suspicious of everything or to believe only what agrees with our personal opinions. We need patience, careful examination, ethical reflection and the willingness to learn from reliable sources.

In a world where information travels faster than ever, the ability to pause and verify is an important expression of mindfulness.

We can listen without immediately believing. We can observe without rushing to judge. We can question without becoming hostile. We can learn without being blindly influenced.

Most importantly, we can take responsibility for what we choose to believe, practise and share.

Before accepting a claim, pause. Before sharing a story, verify. Before reaching a conclusion, reflect. Let mindfulness guide us towards understanding, and let wisdom guide our actions.

🌿 A Moment of Reflection

The next time you receive a surprising message, hear a rumour or encounter a strong opinion, give yourself a moment before reacting.

Ask yourself:

  • Do I know where this information came from?
  • Have I checked whether it is accurate?
  • Am I accepting it because it is supported by evidence, or simply because I agree with it?
  • Could sharing it cause unnecessary harm?
  • What would a thoughtful and wise response look like?

A mindful pause may prevent a careless judgement. A careful examination may prevent misunderstanding. A willingness to learn may bring us closer to wisdom.
