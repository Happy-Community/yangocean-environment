Meditation for Single Adults: Finding Peace and Contentment

Being single is one of the many ways people experience life. Whether we are single by choice, circumstances, or a change in life, we can use this time to understand ourselves more deeply, cultivate inner peace, and build a life guided by kindness, wisdom and purpose.

1. Being Single Does Not Mean Being Incomplete

Society sometimes places great importance on marriage and romantic relationships. As a result, single adults may occasionally feel pressure to follow a particular path or compare their lives with those of others.

Mindfulness invites us to pause and observe these thoughts without immediately believing or judging them. Our worth is not determined by our relationship status. Each person has the potential to learn, grow, care for others and contribute to the world.

A meaningful life can include friendships, family relationships, learning, meaningful work, creativity, service to others and a connection with nature. We do not need to wait for a particular life event before beginning to live well.

A gentle reflection:

Instead of asking, “Why is my life different from others?”, we can ask, “How can I make this life meaningful, kind and peaceful?”

2. Learn to Enjoy Our Own Company

Spending time alone can offer an opportunity to understand our thoughts, feelings and habits. We may discover what genuinely brings us joy, what causes unnecessary worry, and which values we wish to live by.

We can enjoy simple activities mindfully: drinking a cup of tea, reading a book, preparing a meal, tending plants, walking outdoors or listening to the sounds of nature. When we give our full attention to an ordinary moment, it can become an opportunity for calm and appreciation.

However, enjoying solitude does not mean that we must do everything alone. Healthy friendships, family connections and supportive communities remain important. We can value quiet time while also making room for companionship and asking for support when needed.

3. Observe Thoughts Without Being Carried Away

At times, thoughts about the past or future may arise. We may remember a difficult relationship, worry about growing older, wonder about the future, or feel lonely when others are celebrating important occasions.

Mindfulness does not require us to suppress these feelings or pretend that everything is fine. Instead, we learn to recognise what is happening in the present moment.

We can gently acknowledge, “This is sadness,” “This is worry,” or “This is a feeling of loneliness.” We then return our attention to the breath or another present-moment experience.

Feelings naturally change. By observing them with patience rather than reacting immediately, we may create space to respond more wisely and compassionately.

4. Practise Loving-Kindness

In Buddhist meditation, loving-kindness, or mettā, is the cultivation of goodwill towards ourselves and others. It is not limited to people who are married, in relationships or surrounded by family. Everyone can practise it.

We can begin by quietly repeating these phrases:

May I be well and peaceful.

May I learn to meet life's difficulties with wisdom.

May I be free from unnecessary suffering.

May others also experience safety, happiness and peace.

We can gradually extend these wishes to our family, friends, neighbours, people we find difficult, and all living beings. The practice encourages goodwill without requiring others to fulfil our expectations.

5. Build a Life Guided by Values and Purpose

A fulfilling life is not measured only by relationship status, possessions or achievements. We can also reflect on the qualities we develop and the good we bring to others.

Consider making space for activities such as:

  • Kindness and generosity: Offer help when we can, share what is useful, and treat people with respect.
  • Learning and personal growth: Read, develop a skill, or explore a subject that deepens our understanding.
  • Mindful living: Make thoughtful choices about spending, consumption and daily habits.
  • Connection with nature: Walk among trees, grow plants, or care for the environment.
  • Community and friendship: Nurture supportive relationships and participate in activities that bring people together.
  • Rest and self-care: Respect our physical and emotional needs without feeling guilty for taking time to recharge.

We do not need to accomplish everything at once. Small, consistent actions can gradually shape a life that reflects our values.

6. Reflect on Change with Wisdom

Buddhist teachings encourage us to contemplate impermanence: experiences, circumstances, relationships and feelings are subject to change. Recognising this does not mean becoming indifferent to life. Rather, it can help us appreciate what we have while meeting change with greater understanding.

We may not be able to control every circumstance, but we can learn to pay attention to our intentions, actions and responses. Instead of measuring our lives against another person's journey, we can focus on cultivating wisdom, compassion and wholesome qualities.

Being single is not a problem that everyone needs to solve. For some, it is a fulfilling choice; for others, it may be a temporary or difficult circumstance. Whatever our situation, we can approach ourselves and others without shame or judgement.

7. A Simple Daily Meditation Practice

Begin with five to ten minutes.

  1. Find a quiet place and sit in a comfortable, balanced posture. You may sit on a chair if that feels better.
  2. Allow the body to relax while keeping the mind gently attentive.
  3. Notice the natural breath without forcing it to become deeper or slower.
  4. When thoughts or feelings arise, acknowledge them gently and return your attention to the breath.
  5. For the final minute, cultivate gratitude or silently offer wishes of loving-kindness to yourself and others.
  6. Before finishing, consider one small wholesome action you can take during the day.

There is no need to judge a meditation session as successful or unsuccessful. The practice is to begin again patiently, whenever we notice that the mind has wandered.

Closing Reflection: A Life Worth Nurturing

Our lives do not all follow the same timetable. Some people marry, some remain single, some experience changes in relationships, and others find fulfilment in different forms of companionship and community.

Rather than judging ourselves by comparison, we can learn to appreciate the life before us. Through mindfulness, we can better understand our emotions, make wiser choices and cultivate compassion for ourselves and others.

May we learn to live with a peaceful heart, a wise mind and kindness towards all beings. May our lives be meaningful not because they look like someone else's, but because of the care, understanding and goodness we bring to each day.
