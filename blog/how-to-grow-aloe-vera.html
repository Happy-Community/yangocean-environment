🌿 How to Grow Aloe Vera and Use Its Clear Gel

Aloe vera is a hardy plant that can grow in a pot or garden with relatively little care. Here, we explore how to grow it, learn about its traditional uses, and understand the clear gel inside its leaves.

🌱 Why Grow Aloe Vera?

Aloe vera is a succulent plant known for its thick, fleshy leaves and ability to store water. It can be a useful addition to a home garden, balcony or sunny windowsill.

Growing a plant ourselves gives us an opportunity to learn about nature, care for a living thing and appreciate the many ways plants have been used in traditional practices.

🪴 Five Simple Steps to Grow Aloe Vera

1. Choose a healthy plant

Start with a healthy aloe vera plant or a well-established offshoot. Choose leaves that look firm and green rather than soft, damaged or badly discoloured.

2. Use a pot with drainage

Select a pot with drainage holes. Aloe vera does not like to sit in water, so good drainage is important.

3. Choose suitable soil

Use a well-draining cactus or succulent potting mix. Heavy soil that stays wet for a long time can cause the roots to rot.

4. Provide bright light

Place the plant where it receives plenty of bright light. If you move it from shade to stronger sunlight, do so gradually to help prevent leaf scorch.

5. Water carefully

Allow the soil to dry out between waterings. Water thoroughly, then let excess water drain away. Water less frequently during cooler or less sunny periods.

Growing tip: Aloe vera generally needs less frequent watering than many other houseplants. The condition of the soil is a better guide than following a fixed watering schedule.

🌿 Aloe Vera: A Useful Plant with a Long History

Aloe vera has a long history of use in traditional skin care and other household practices in different parts of the world. Today, aloe-derived ingredients are also used in a variety of commercial skin-care products.

The clear, jelly-like material inside the leaf is commonly called aloe vera gel. People may use aloe-containing products to soothe or moisturise the skin. However, results vary, and traditional use does not mean that every use has been proven effective by modern research.

Learning about a plant can help us appreciate both traditional knowledge and the importance of using it thoughtfully.

💚 My Personal Experience with Aloe Vera

In my own experience, I have found the clear inner gel helpful when I had a mouth ulcer. I have also used aloe vera as part of my personal care for concerns such as itchy skin, freckles and corns.

These are my personal experiences, not a promise that aloe vera will produce the same results for everyone. Skin concerns can have different causes, and a change in symptoms does not necessarily establish that aloe vera was responsible.

If a mouth ulcer does not heal, a corn is painful or persistent, skin itching continues, or a spot changes in appearance, seek advice from a qualified healthcare professional.

🔎 The Clear Inner Gel: Which Part Is Commonly Used?

An aloe vera leaf has an outer green skin and a clear, jelly-like inner portion. Between the outer skin and the inner gel, a yellowish sap may be present.

  • Clear inner gel: This is the part commonly used in many topical aloe vera preparations and skin-care products.
  • Green outer skin: This is generally removed when preparing the inner gel.
  • Yellow sap or latex: This is different from the clear gel and may contain compounds with a strong laxative effect if swallowed.

Separating these parts carefully is important. The appearance of a clear gel does not by itself guarantee that a freshly cut leaf is safe for every use.

🧼 How to Prepare Fresh Aloe Vera Gel for Topical Use

If you choose to prepare aloe vera for external use, take care with the leaf and avoid contact with the yellow sap.

  1. Choose a healthy leaf. Use a clean knife and cut a mature outer leaf from a healthy plant.
  2. Let the yellow sap drain. Hold the cut end downward and allow the yellowish sap to drain away.
  3. Wash the leaf. Rinse the outside carefully under clean running water.
  4. Remove the green skin. Place the leaf on a clean cutting board and carefully peel away the outer skin.
  5. Separate the clear gel. Remove the transparent inner gel without including the green skin or yellow sap.
  6. Rinse the gel. Rinse it with clean water before topical use.
Use with care: Try a small amount on a small area of skin first. Stop using it if redness, burning, itching or other irritation occurs. Do not apply it to deep wounds or serious burns without medical guidance.

🥄 Can Aloe Vera Be Eaten or Drunk?

Aloe vera products made for consumption are not necessarily the same as freshly cut leaves. Some commercially prepared products are processed to remove unwanted components, but product quality and preparation methods can differ.

Do not assume that raw aloe vera is safe to eat simply because it comes directly from a plant. The yellow latex can cause abdominal cramps and diarrhoea, and swallowing aloe latex may present additional health risks.

Cooking a whole leaf does not guarantee that it becomes safe to consume. If you are considering an aloe vera product for eating or drinking, choose a product specifically intended for that purpose and follow its instructions.

If you take medication, have a medical condition, are pregnant or breastfeeding, ask a qualified healthcare professional before consuming aloe vera products.

🌱 Growing as a Mindful Practice

Caring for aloe vera reminds us that living things have their own needs and pace. A plant does not need constant attention; it needs the right conditions and thoughtful care.

As we observe new leaves and healthy growth, we can also practise patience, gratitude and respect for nature.

A small plant can be a gentle reminder to care for the world around us, one day at a time.
