Living Mindfully: Keeping Our Minds Clear and Our Senses Awake

Life brings us many experiences: happiness and disappointment, comfort and discomfort, success and uncertainty. When difficulties arise, we may sometimes wish to escape our feelings or find a quick way to make them disappear.

Mindful living invites us to pause and notice what is happening within us. Rather than automatically reacting or trying to avoid every uncomfortable experience, we can learn to meet life with greater awareness, patience and understanding.

1. Why a Clear Mind Matters

Our state of mind can influence how we think, speak and act. When our awareness is clouded, we may find it harder to recognise the consequences of our actions, understand our emotions or make thoughtful decisions.

Keeping our minds clear does not mean that we must always feel peaceful, happy or free from difficult thoughts. It means allowing ourselves to recognise what we are experiencing and responding as wisely as we can.

A clear mind gives us more room to pause before reacting, consider different perspectives and choose actions that reflect our values.

2. The Buddhist Fifth Precept: Avoiding Intoxicants

In Buddhist teachings, the fifth precept encourages abstaining from intoxicating drinks and drugs that lead to heedlessness. Its purpose is closely connected with protecting awareness and cultivating a mind that can recognise actions and their consequences.

We do not need to identify as Buddhist to reflect on the value behind this principle. In everyday life, we can ask ourselves whether our choices support clear awareness, responsible behaviour and consideration for ourselves and others.

Alcohol and other intoxicating substances can impair judgement and awareness. Avoiding them can be one way to support a more mindful way of living.

This reflection is not about judging or shaming anyone. People have different circumstances and may face challenges with substance use. Compassion, appropriate support and practical steps towards change are more helpful than blame.

3. Are We Trying to Escape What We Feel?

Sometimes, the wish to numb ourselves arises when we feel stressed, lonely, disappointed, anxious or overwhelmed. We may want to stop thinking about a problem or avoid feelings that seem too difficult to face.

Wanting relief is a human response. We all need rest and comfort at times. However, it can be helpful to notice the difference between taking a healthy break and repeatedly relying on something that clouds our awareness or prevents us from addressing what is troubling us.

Instead of judging ourselves for wanting to escape, we can gently ask: What am I feeling right now? What do I need? Is there a healthier way to care for myself?

4. Keeping Our Senses Awake in Everyday Life

Keeping our senses awake means being present with the world around us and aware of our own experience. We can practise this in simple, ordinary moments.

  • When eating: Notice the taste, smell and texture of our food instead of rushing through the meal.
  • When walking outdoors: Observe the trees, feel the breeze and listen to the sounds around us.
  • When emotions arise: Recognise feelings without immediately acting on every impulse.
  • When using digital devices: Notice whether we are using them intentionally or simply avoiding an uncomfortable moment.
  • When making decisions: Pause to consider how our choices may affect ourselves and other people.

These small moments of awareness help us reconnect with our experiences. We become more able to appreciate what is happening now instead of constantly seeking distraction.

5. Healthy Rest Is Not the Same as Avoidance

Mindful living does not mean that we must confront every problem immediately or remain serious all the time. Rest, laughter, music, hobbies, time in nature and conversations with people we trust can all be part of a balanced life.

Sometimes we need to step away from a difficult situation before we can understand it more clearly. A restful break can help us regain perspective and return with renewed energy.

The important question is whether our habits help us recover and live with greater awareness, or whether they repeatedly leave us less able to understand ourselves and respond to life.

If substance use has become difficult to control, seeking support from a qualified healthcare professional or an appropriate support service can be a meaningful step. We do not have to face every difficulty alone.

6. A Simple Practice: Pause, Notice and Choose

When we notice an urge to react, escape or dull our awareness, we can try a simple pause.

  1. Pause. Give ourselves a moment before acting on the urge, if it is safe to do so.
  2. Notice. Recognise the thoughts, feelings and bodily sensations present in this moment.
  3. Understand. Ask what may be contributing to this experience and what kind of care we need.
  4. Choose. Take one small step that supports our well-being, awareness and responsibilities.

We may not always respond perfectly. Mindfulness is not about demanding perfection from ourselves. It is about noticing, learning and beginning again with patience.

Reflection: Are We Living with Awareness?

Take a quiet moment to reflect on these questions:

  • When life feels difficult, how do we usually respond?
  • Do our everyday habits help us remain aware, or do they sometimes become ways of avoiding our feelings?
  • What healthy activities help us feel refreshed and reconnect with ourselves?
  • What is one small choice we can make today to support a clearer mind?

Living with Clarity, Balance and Compassion

Keeping our minds clear and our senses awake is an ongoing practice. It begins with recognising our experiences honestly, understanding our intentions and making choices with care.

By cultivating awareness, we can learn to appreciate life's pleasant moments without constantly chasing them, and face difficult moments without always needing to escape them.

We do not need to change everything at once. Each mindful pause, each thoughtful decision and each act of compassion can become part of a more conscious way of living.

May we learn to care for our minds, remain awake to life's experiences and meet ourselves and others with wisdom and kindness.
