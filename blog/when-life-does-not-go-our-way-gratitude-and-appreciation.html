When Life Does Not Go Our Way: The Practice of Gratitude and Appreciation

Learning to acknowledge disappointment while remembering the good that remains in our lives.

Life does not always unfold according to our wishes. We may work hard towards a goal and still fail to achieve it. Someone may disappoint us, a plan may fall apart, or circumstances may prevent us from doing what we hoped to do. When these things happen, it is natural to feel hurt, frustrated, sad or discouraged.

However, when we focus entirely on what has gone wrong, we may overlook the many things that are still going well. Mindfulness invites us to pause, observe our feelings and look at our circumstances with a wider perspective. We can acknowledge our difficulties without allowing them to overshadow everything good in our lives.

1. Remember That Things Could Be Worse

When we face disappointment, our minds can become absorbed in the problem. We may repeatedly ask ourselves why something happened, why another person behaved in a certain way, or why life did not turn out as we expected. The more we dwell on these thoughts, the heavier our disappointment may become.

Sometimes, it helps to step back and consider the wider picture. Although our present situation may be difficult, there may still be things we can appreciate: a safe place to rest, food to eat, someone who cares about us, the ability to learn, or another opportunity to try again.

Remembering that circumstances could be worse is not about comparing suffering or telling ourselves that we have no right to feel sad. Our difficulties deserve to be acknowledged. Rather, this reflection helps us recognise that a painful experience is only one part of our lives, not the whole of our lives.

A mindful reminder: “This is difficult, and I can acknowledge that. At the same time, what is still good in my life that I may have overlooked?”

2. Do Not Let Disappointment Blind Us to What We Have

It is easy to take familiar blessings for granted. We may become so concerned about what we lack that we forget to appreciate what we already have. We may wish for a better home, a more successful career, greater recognition or a life that seems easier than our own.

Yet, while pursuing improvements is not necessarily wrong, we can lose our peace when our happiness depends entirely on getting something more or making everything happen according to our preferences.

Appreciation helps us notice ordinary goodness: a thoughtful message from a friend, a meal shared with family, a moment of quiet, the beauty of a tree, or the chance to begin again after a difficult day. These moments may appear small, but noticing them can help us live with greater contentment.

3. Accept What We Cannot Always Control

We can make plans, work diligently and treat others with kindness, but we cannot control every outcome. Other people have their own thoughts, decisions and circumstances. Conditions can also change in ways we did not anticipate.

When things do not go our way, it may be helpful to distinguish between what we can change and what we cannot. If there is a practical step we can take, we can take it calmly. If something is beyond our control, we can practise letting go of the repeated struggle against reality.

Acceptance does not mean giving up, approving of harmful behaviour or refusing to solve a problem. It means seeing the situation clearly enough to decide what can be done, without adding unnecessary suffering through endless resentment or regret.

4. Practise Gratitude Even During Difficult Times

Gratitude is easy to feel when life is comfortable. It can be more challenging when we are disappointed, worried or facing uncertainty. During these times, we do not need to force ourselves to feel cheerful or pretend that everything is fine.

Instead, we can begin with something small and genuine. We might appreciate a person who listened to us, the strength to get through a difficult day, the lesson gained from an experience, or the simple fact that we have another moment to reflect and choose our next step.

Gratitude does not erase pain. It allows pain and appreciation to exist together. We can feel disappointed about one part of our lives while remaining thankful for other parts.

5. Reflect on Impermanence

In Buddhist practice, reflecting on impermanence helps us understand that experiences, feelings and circumstances do not remain fixed forever. Pleasant situations change, and difficult situations can also change. Our present disappointment, however strong it feels, is not necessarily how we will feel for the rest of our lives.

When we remember that conditions are changing, we may become less attached to the expectation that everything must happen exactly as we wish. We can learn from our experiences while recognising that no single outcome defines our entire life.

This reflection is not a promise that every problem will resolve in the way we want. Rather, it encourages us to meet changing circumstances with awareness, patience and wisdom.

6. Appreciate the People Around Us

Disappointment involving other people can be especially painful. We may feel hurt when someone does not meet our expectations or fails to respond in the way we hoped. When this happens, it is useful to acknowledge our feelings and consider the situation carefully before reacting.

We can communicate our needs, establish healthy boundaries and address problems when necessary. At the same time, we can remember the kindness and support that others have offered us. Appreciating someone's good qualities does not require us to ignore their mistakes or accept behaviour that harms us.

We can also express appreciation directly. A sincere thank-you, a thoughtful message or a small act of kindness can remind both ourselves and others that relationships are not only about what we expect to receive, but also about what we can give.

7. Turn Difficulties into Opportunities for Growth

Some disappointments reveal that our plans need to change. Others teach us about patience, our expectations or the importance of preparing for uncertainty. We may discover that our happiness has become too dependent on recognition, success or the approval of others.

We do not have to be grateful for every painful event or pretend that suffering is always beneficial. Nevertheless, when we are ready, we can ask what the experience may teach us and what we might do differently in the future.

Perhaps we can develop a more realistic plan, seek advice, take better care of ourselves or become more understanding towards others who are struggling. In this way, we can respond to difficulty constructively instead of allowing disappointment to define our outlook.

8. A Simple Mindfulness Practice: Pause, Reflect and Appreciate

The next time something does not go according to your wishes, try this simple practice. Take a few quiet moments before responding.

  1. Pause. Take a few gentle breaths. Notice your feelings without immediately judging yourself or blaming someone else.
  2. Acknowledge. Name what is happening: “I feel disappointed,” “I feel hurt,” or “This is not what I expected.”
  3. Look at the wider picture. Ask yourself what remains good in your life, even while this problem exists.
  4. Appreciate. Identify one person, experience or simple condition for which you can sincerely feel thankful.
  5. Choose your next step. Consider what you can do constructively. If there is nothing useful to do immediately, allow yourself time to settle before deciding.

This practice does not require us to solve every problem at once. Its purpose is to create a little space between what happens and how we respond, helping us act with greater awareness rather than being carried away by our first reaction.

Questions for reflection

What am I disappointed about today? What am I still able to appreciate? Is there something within my control that I can improve? Can I meet the rest of this day with a little more patience, gratitude and kindness?

Conclusion: Let Gratitude Widen Our Perspective

Life will not always meet our expectations. There will be disappointments, misunderstandings, missed opportunities and circumstances we cannot change. We do not need to deny our pain or pretend that these experiences do not matter.

What we can practise is remembering that our difficulties are not the only things present in our lives. Alongside them, there may be kindness, learning, friendship, nature, simple comforts and opportunities to begin again.

When we learn to acknowledge what hurts while appreciating what remains, we give ourselves a more balanced way to meet life's uncertainties. Gratitude does not make every difficulty disappear, but it can help us avoid overlooking the good that still exists.

May we learn to accept what we cannot always control, improve what we can, appreciate the kindness we receive and offer kindness to others. Even when life does not go our way, we can continue to cultivate awareness, contentment and a grateful heart.
