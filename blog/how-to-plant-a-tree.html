🌳 How to Plant a Tree

Planting a tree is a simple action, but it is also a long-term commitment. A young tree may take years to mature, yet the care we give it today can benefit people, wildlife and the surrounding environment for many years to come.

You do not need to be an expert gardener to plant a tree. What matters most is choosing a suitable tree, giving it a good place to grow, planting it correctly and caring for it while it becomes established.

This guide provides a simple starting point.

🌱 Step 1 — Choose the Right Tree

Start by choosing a healthy tree that is suitable for your local climate and growing conditions.

Where appropriate, consider native or locally suitable species. They may be better adapted to local conditions and can provide food or habitat for local wildlife.

Think about the tree's mature size before planting it. A small sapling can eventually become a very large tree.

Think ahead:

Check the distance from buildings, walls, power lines, roads, drains and underground services before deciding where to plant.

☀️ Step 2 — Choose a Suitable Location

Observe the area where you intend to plant. Consider sunlight, available space, soil condition and access to water.

Different trees have different requirements. Some prefer full sun while others tolerate partial shade. Some need more space for their roots and branches than others.

A good location gives the tree enough room to grow without causing problems for nearby structures or other plants.

🪴 Step 3 — Prepare the Tree

If the tree is growing in a container, water it before planting if the growing medium is dry.

Remove the container carefully and try to keep the root ball together.

If roots are tightly circling the outside of the root ball, gently loosen them where appropriate. Avoid damaging healthy roots unnecessarily.

⛏️ Step 4 — Dig the Planting Hole

Dig a hole that is wide enough for the roots to spread naturally. The hole should generally not be deeper than the tree's root ball.

The surrounding soil should remain firm enough to support the tree. Avoid creating a deep hole that causes the tree to settle too far below the surrounding ground level.

🌳 Step 5 — Place the Tree

Place the tree in the centre of the hole. Check that it is standing upright.

The top of the root ball should be level with, or slightly above, the surrounding soil. Do not bury the trunk.

Important:

The trunk should remain above the soil. Planting too deeply can interfere with the tree's health.

🌱 Step 6 — Fill the Hole

Return the soil around the root ball. Fill the space gradually and gently firm the soil around the roots.

There is usually no need to compact the soil heavily. The aim is to remove large air pockets while still allowing water and air to move through the soil.

💧 Step 7 — Water Thoroughly

Water the newly planted tree thoroughly so that the soil settles around the roots.

Continue to provide appropriate water while the tree becomes established. Pay particular attention during hot or dry periods.

The exact amount and frequency of watering depends on the tree, soil, weather and local conditions.

Mindful watering:

Rather than watering automatically every day, observe the soil and weather and give the tree water when it needs it.

🍂 Step 8 — Add Mulch Carefully

A suitable layer of organic mulch around the planting area can help retain soil moisture and reduce competition from weeds.

Leave some space around the trunk. Do not pile mulch directly against the tree.

🌿 Step 9 — Continue Caring for the Tree

Planting day is only the beginning. Observe the tree regularly as it becomes established.

Watch for signs of water stress, damaged branches, pests, disease or competition from surrounding vegetation.

Give the tree appropriate care without disturbing it unnecessarily. As it grows stronger, its need for intensive establishment care will generally change.

⚠️ A Few Things to Avoid

  • Do not plant a tree without considering its eventual size.
  • Do not bury the trunk or root flare too deeply.
  • Do not compact the planting soil excessively.
  • Do not allow mulch to touch the trunk.
  • Do not assume every tree needs the same amount of water.
  • Do not forget that newly planted trees need care while their root systems become established.

🪴 What If You Don't Have a Garden?

Not everyone has enough space to plant a tree. That does not mean you cannot grow something.

A balcony, small garden, window area or suitable container can provide an opportunity to care for a living plant. Herbs and other useful plants can be a good place to begin.

For example, aloe vera can be grown in a container with suitable drainage and growing conditions.

Growing a small plant does not replace the ecological role of a tree, but it can help us develop the habit of observing, caring and using natural resources thoughtfully.

🌍 A Tree Is a Long-Term Gift

When we plant a tree, we may not be the person who enjoys all of its benefits.

Someone else may one day sit in its shade. Birds may find shelter in its branches. Insects may visit its flowers. Its fallen leaves may return nutrients to the soil.

This is one reason planting a tree can be more than a gardening activity. It can be an act of care for a future we may not see.

We do not need to plant hundreds of trees to begin. Sometimes one healthy tree, planted in the right place and cared for well, is a meaningful beginning.
