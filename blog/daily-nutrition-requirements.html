🥗 Daily Nutrition Requirements for Women and Men

A Vegetarian Guide to Balanced Eating

Discover how a variety of plant-based foods can provide important nutrients to support our everyday health and well-being.

Listen to the main article content.

Read Aloud uses a voice available in your browser.

🌱 Why Daily Nutrition Matters

Our bodies need a variety of nutrients every day to support normal functions, maintain energy and protect overall health. These nutrients include carbohydrates, protein, healthy fats, vitamins, minerals, fibre and water.

A vegetarian diet can provide many essential nutrients through vegetables, fruits, legumes, whole grains, soy products, nuts and seeds. However, eating a variety of foods is important because no single food provides everything our bodies need.

Women and men share many nutritional requirements, but their needs may differ according to body size, age, activity level, reproductive stage and individual health circumstances.

A balanced diet is not about eating large amounts of food or following strict rules. It is about choosing a suitable variety of nourishing foods and enjoying them in appropriate portions.

🥬 Essential Nutrients for Everyday Health

1. Protein – Supporting Muscles and Body Repair

Protein helps build and maintain muscles and supports the repair of body tissues. It is also needed to make enzymes and other important substances in the body.

Vegetarian sources of protein include:

  • Tofu and tempeh
  • Soybeans and edamame
  • Lentils and chickpeas
  • Kidney beans, black beans and other legumes
  • Peanuts, almonds and other nuts
  • Pumpkin seeds, sunflower seeds and chia seeds
  • Quinoa, oats and other whole grains

Including a protein-rich food in each main meal can help us obtain protein throughout the day. Individual protein needs depend on body weight, age, activity level and health.

2. Iron – Supporting Healthy Blood

Iron is needed to make haemoglobin, a protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen around the body.

Plant-based sources of iron include:

  • Lentils, chickpeas and beans
  • Tofu and tempeh
  • Spinach and other dark green vegetables
  • Pumpkin seeds and sesame seeds
  • Iron-fortified breakfast cereals
  • Whole grains and dried apricots

Iron from plant foods is not absorbed as easily as iron from some animal foods. Eating vitamin C sources such as oranges, guava, tomatoes, kiwi fruit or bell peppers alongside iron-rich foods can improve iron absorption.

Women who menstruate generally need more iron than men because of monthly blood loss. Pregnancy may also increase iron requirements.

3. Calcium – Supporting Bones and Teeth

Calcium is important for strong bones and teeth. It also plays a role in muscle contraction and nerve function.

Vegetarian sources of calcium include:

  • Calcium-fortified soy milk and plant drinks
  • Calcium-set tofu
  • Calcium-fortified plant-based yoghurt
  • Kale, bok choy and selected leafy vegetables
  • Sesame seeds and almonds
  • Calcium-fortified breakfast cereals

Check product labels because calcium content varies between tofu products and fortified plant-based drinks.

4. Vitamin B12 – Supporting Nerves and Blood Cells

Vitamin B12 is needed for normal nerve function and the formation of red blood cells. It deserves particular attention in vegetarian and especially vegan diets.

Reliable vegetarian sources may include vitamin B12-fortified plant milk, fortified breakfast cereals and fortified nutritional yeast.

Unfortified plant foods are generally not reliable sources of vitamin B12. People following a fully plant-based diet should ensure they have a dependable fortified-food or supplement source.

5. Vitamin D – Supporting Bones and Muscles

Vitamin D helps the body absorb calcium and supports bone and muscle health.

Vegetarian-friendly sources include:

  • Vitamin D-fortified plant milk
  • Vitamin D-fortified breakfast cereals
  • UV-exposed mushrooms, where available
  • Vitamin D supplements when recommended

Sunlight helps the body produce vitamin D, although the amount produced varies according to factors such as skin exposure, time of day and individual circumstances.

6. Omega-3 Fatty Acids – Supporting Overall Health

Omega-3 fatty acids are important fats that our bodies need. Plant-based sources mainly provide alpha-linolenic acid (ALA).

Vegetarian sources include:

  • Chia seeds
  • Ground flaxseeds
  • Walnuts
  • Hemp seeds
  • Canola and soybean oils

The body converts only a limited amount of ALA into the longer-chain omega-3 fatty acids EPA and DHA. Vegetarian algae-based omega-3 products can provide another source of these fatty acids.

7. Fibre – Supporting Digestive Health

Dietary fibre supports normal digestion and helps maintain bowel regularity. Fibre-rich foods can also contribute to a feeling of fullness.

Good sources include:

  • Oats and whole-grain bread
  • Brown rice and other whole grains
  • Beans, lentils and chickpeas
  • Apples, pears, berries and guava
  • Broccoli, carrots and leafy vegetables
  • Nuts and seeds

Increase fibre gradually and drink enough water to support digestion.

8. Zinc and Iodine – Important Minerals

Zinc supports immune function, wound healing and many processes in the body. Vegetarian sources include beans, tofu, oats, nuts, pumpkin seeds and whole grains.

Iodine is needed for the production of thyroid hormones. Suitable sources can include iodised salt and selected fortified foods. Seaweed contains iodine, but its content can vary greatly, and some varieties contain excessive amounts.

👩‍🦰👨 Nutritional Considerations for Women and Men

Women and men need the same broad categories of nutrients, but the amounts required may differ. Age, body size, activity level and health circumstances all influence daily needs.

👩 Nutritional Considerations for Women

  • Iron: Women who menstruate generally need more iron to replace monthly blood losses.
  • Folate: Important for normal cell division. Women who may become pregnant should follow appropriate medical guidance on folic acid.
  • Calcium and vitamin D: Important throughout life for maintaining bone health.
  • Protein: Supports muscles and body repair, with needs varying according to body weight and activity.
  • Vitamin B12: Particularly important when following a fully plant-based diet.

👨 Nutritional Considerations for Men

  • Protein: Supports muscle maintenance and repair, particularly for physically active individuals.
  • Zinc: Supports immune function and normal body processes.
  • Magnesium: Involved in muscle and nerve function and energy production.
  • Vitamin B12: Supports normal nerve function and red blood cell formation.
  • Fibre and potassium: Important parts of a varied diet containing fruits, vegetables, legumes and whole grains.

These are general nutritional considerations, not a complete list of individual requirements. Women and men should choose foods according to their own circumstances rather than assuming that everyone of the same sex needs an identical diet.

🍽️ A One-Day Vegetarian Meal Plan

The following is an example of how we can combine different plant-based foods throughout one day. Portions should be adjusted according to age, appetite, body size, activity level and individual nutritional needs.

🌅 Breakfast

  • Oatmeal prepared with fortified soy milk
  • Banana or berries
  • Chia seeds or ground flaxseeds
  • A small handful of walnuts or almonds

This combination provides carbohydrates, fibre, plant protein and healthy fats. Fortified soy milk may also provide calcium, vitamin D and vitamin B12, depending on the product.

🍊 Morning Snack

  • An orange, guava or kiwi fruit
  • A small handful of pumpkin seeds

Fruit provides vitamin C and other beneficial nutrients, while pumpkin seeds contribute minerals and protein.

☀️ Lunch

  • Brown rice or mixed-grain rice
  • Stir-fried tofu or tempeh
  • Broccoli, carrots and bok choy
  • Lentil or bean soup

This meal combines plant protein, whole grains, vegetables and fibre. Adding tomatoes or bell peppers can provide vitamin C to accompany iron-containing plant foods.

🍎 Afternoon Snack

  • An apple or pear
  • Plain soy yoghurt or fortified plant yoghurt

A simple snack can help provide additional nutrients between meals. Check product labels for added sugar and fortification.

🌙 Dinner

  • Quinoa or brown rice
  • Chickpea and vegetable curry
  • Steamed spinach or other leafy vegetables
  • A small serving of avocado

Chickpeas provide plant protein and fibre, while vegetables contribute vitamins and minerals. Avocado provides unsaturated fats.

💧 Throughout the Day

Drink water regularly according to your needs, activity and environment. Unsweetened drinks can also be part of a balanced daily routine.

🌈 Building a Balanced Vegetarian Plate

A simple way to plan meals is to include different food groups rather than relying on only one type of food.

  • Vegetables and fruits: Include a variety of colours and types.
  • Whole grains: Choose oats, brown rice, whole-grain bread, millet or quinoa.
  • Plant protein: Include tofu, tempeh, beans, lentils or chickpeas.
  • Healthy fats: Add nuts, seeds, avocado or suitable plant oils.
  • Fortified foods: Consider fortified plant milk and other products to help meet specific nutrient needs.

The World Health Organization recommends that people older than 10 years aim for at least 400 grams of fruits and vegetables daily and at least 25 grams of naturally occurring dietary fibre. These are useful general targets, although individual requirements can vary.

💚 Important Reminders for Vegetarian Nutrition

  1. Eat a wide variety of vegetables, fruits, legumes, whole grains, nuts and seeds.
  2. Include a source of protein in your main meals.
  3. Pay particular attention to vitamin B12 if you avoid all animal-derived foods.
  4. Combine iron-rich plant foods with vitamin C-rich fruits or vegetables.
  5. Choose fortified foods where appropriate and check their nutritional labels.
  6. Limit excessive salt, added sugar and highly processed foods.
  7. Drink enough water and maintain regular physical activity.

Supplements are not automatically necessary for everyone. However, some people may need them depending on their diet, life stage, medical circumstances or blood test results. Seek advice from a qualified healthcare professional or registered dietitian before starting supplements.

🌿 Nourishing Our Bodies with Thoughtful Choices

A balanced vegetarian diet can offer a wide variety of nourishing foods. From colourful vegetables and fresh fruits to tofu, beans, lentils, whole grains, nuts and seeds, there are many ways to enjoy meals while providing our bodies with important nutrients.

Women and men may have different nutritional needs at different stages of life, but the foundation remains similar: variety, balance, moderation and consistency.

We do not need to make every meal complicated. Small, thoughtful choices each day can help us build a healthier relationship with food.

Let us appreciate the nourishment that nature provides and make food choices with awareness, gratitude and care.

📌 Health Information Disclaimer

This article provides general educational information and is not a personalised nutrition plan or medical advice. Nutritional requirements vary according to age, body size, activity level, pregnancy, health conditions and other individual factors. People with specific dietary needs or concerns should consult a qualified healthcare professional or registered dietitian.
