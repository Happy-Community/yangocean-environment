🌫️ Can Trees Help Counter Haze?

Trees support biodiversity, provide shade and can capture some airborne particles. However, haze caused by forest fires, peatland fires and other pollution cannot be cancelled simply by planting a certain number of trees.

This calculator helps you plan a tree-planting target. It does not calculate how many trees would remove haze.

🌱 Plan Your Tree-Planting Target

Enter the number of established trees you hope to have. Choose an assumed seedling survival rate to estimate how many seedlings you may need to plant.

The survival rate is an assumption, not a guarantee. Actual survival depends on species, location, weather, planting conditions and ongoing care.

Your estimate will appear here.

Example: for a target of 100 established trees and an assumed survival rate of 70%, the estimate is 143 seedlings.

🌿 What Can We Do During Haze?

  • Check Malaysia's official Air Pollutant Index (API).
  • Follow public-health advice and reduce outdoor exposure when necessary.
  • Avoid open burning and support fire-prevention efforts.
  • Protect existing forests and peatlands.
  • Plant suitable trees in appropriate locations and care for them as they grow.

Check Malaysia's Air Pollutant Index (API) →

Remember: tree planting is a long-term environmental investment. During an active haze event, reducing smoke emissions and protecting people from exposure are more immediate priorities.

🌱 Plant the Right Tree in the Right Place

Successful tree planting is about more than the number of seedlings. Choose locally suitable species, avoid damaging natural habitats, and make a plan to water and care for young trees. Protecting mature trees and existing forests is also important.

The calculator is a simple planning aid, not a prediction of long-term tree survival, carbon removal or haze reduction.
