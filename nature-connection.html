🌳 Connect with Nature

Notice trees, plants, birds, insects, light, rain and small changes around you. A connection with nature can begin with something as simple as paying attention to what is already near your home.

🍃 Why Notice Nature Nearby?

We often imagine that connecting with nature requires a forest, mountain, large park or a journey to somewhere far away. Yet nature is also present in the places we see every day.

A roadside tree, a small garden, a bird on a rooftop, an insect visiting a flower or new leaves appearing after rain can all give us a reason to pause and pay attention.

When we regularly notice small details, familiar places can become more interesting and meaningful.

🌱 Grow Something Small

Connecting with nature is not only about observing wild places. Growing something also gives us an opportunity to watch life change over time.

You do not need a large garden. A pot, container or small growing space can be enough to begin.

🌿 Start Small

Choose a plant that suits your space, available light and local conditions. Easy-to-grow herbs or plants can be a simple starting point.

☀️ Notice the Light

Observe how much sunlight reaches the growing area and how the plant responds to its surroundings.

💧 Understand Its Needs

Water according to the plant's needs and the conditions around it. Different plants do not all require the same amount of water.

📷 Record Changes

Take photographs or make short notes from time to time. New leaves, flowers and other changes can be easier to appreciate when you compare them over time.

📓 Start a Simple Nature Journal

You do not need special equipment or scientific knowledge to keep a nature journal. A phone camera and a few simple notes are enough.

Each observation could include:

  • 📅 Date of observation
  • 📍 General area or neighbourhood
  • 🌳 What you noticed
  • ☀️ Weather or time of day
  • 📷 Photographs
  • 🐦 Birds, insects or other wildlife you saw
  • 🍃 Changes since an earlier observation
  • 💭 Personal thoughts or reflections

Over time, these simple records can become a personal record of how the natural world changes around you.

🌳 Get to Know a Tree

Choose a tree that you often pass. Instead of simply walking past it, stop for a moment and look more closely.

🌿 Leaves

Notice their shape, size, colour and texture. Are there new leaves, older leaves or leaves that are changing?

🌸 Flowers & Fruit

Look for flowers, seeds or fruit. These parts of a tree may attract birds, insects and other forms of life.

🪵 Bark & Trunk

Observe the colour, texture and shape of the trunk and branches. Different trees can have very distinctive features.

🐜 Small Visitors

Look closely for ants, beetles, spiders, caterpillars and other small creatures living on or around the tree.

📷 Photograph What You Notice

Photography can help us slow down. Instead of taking a photograph only because something looks beautiful, try recording small details that you might normally overlook.

  • 🌱 New leaves
  • 🌸 Flowers beginning to open
  • 🍂 Fallen leaves with interesting patterns
  • 🐛 Insects visiting plants
  • 🌧️ Water droplets after rain
  • 🌤️ Changes in light around a familiar tree
  • 🌳 The same tree at different times or on different days
  • 🌱 Plants that you are growing

The aim is not to create perfect photographs. The aim is to look more closely.

👀 Take a Five-Minute Nature Pause

When you are outside, try this simple observation exercise.

  1. Stop somewhere safe and comfortable.
  2. Look around without immediately checking your phone.
  3. Notice three things that you can see.
  4. Notice two sounds around you.
  5. Find one small thing you have not noticed before.
  6. Take a photograph if you wish.
  7. Write one sentence about the experience.

Five minutes may seem short, but regular observation can change the way we see familiar places.

🌦️ Watch Nature Change

Nature is always changing, even when the changes are subtle.

A tree may produce new leaves, flowers may appear and disappear, birds may visit at different times, and rain can change the appearance of plants and their surroundings.

Photographing the same place or plant regularly can help us notice changes that are easy to miss when we only look occasionally.

💚 Observe Without Disturbing

Connecting with nature also means respecting it.

  • 🌿 Avoid damaging plants or picking flowers.
  • 🐦 Keep an appropriate distance from wildlife.
  • 🦋 Avoid disturbing nests, insects or other animals.
  • 🚯 Take rubbish home or dispose of it properly.
  • 📍 Avoid sharing exact locations of sensitive wildlife habitats.

Good observation leaves a place as it was or, whenever possible, a little cleaner.

🌱 You Don't Need to Be a Botanist

It is perfectly fine not to know the name of every tree, flower or insect. Observation can come before identification.

You can begin with simple questions:

  • What does it look like?
  • What has changed since I last saw it?
  • What living things visit it?
  • What does it look like after rain?
  • What makes this plant or tree interesting to me?

If you later want to identify a species, that can become part of the learning process.

🍃 Look Closer

Nature does not have to look extraordinary to be appreciated.

Sometimes a connection with nature begins with a tree, a flower, a plant we are growing, a bird or simply a quiet moment outside.

Look closer. Notice more. Appreciate the nature that is already around us.
